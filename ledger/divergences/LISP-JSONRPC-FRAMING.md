## Observable by a package: GNU Lisp written for the engine

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LISP-JSONRPC-FRAMING | jsonrpc.el's Content-Length framing is the process layer's | `jsonrpc--process-filter` inserts each chunk into the connection's ` *NAME output*` buffer, searches it for a `Content-Length` header (within 100 characters) and parses the body from the buffer once it has come, keeping the expected length in the connection's `-expected-bytes` slot | `jsonrpc-process-connection` sets `(set-process-framing PROC 'content-length)` (`lisp/vm/subprocess.el`), so the process layer splits the frames as it reads (`crates/romacs-vm/src/framing.rs`) and the filter is called once a read with the bodies it completed, each parsed by `jsonrpc--json-read`, now a function of the body string. The ` *NAME output*` buffer is still made and still ends the filter's work when killed, but no output is inserted in it; the `-expected-bytes` slot is gone; a header block with no readable Content-Length (a server's stray output) is skipped, where GNU's search may skip it or wait; one over 8 KiB ends the framing of that connection with a warning. The bodies wait in a queue on the process and are read in order: a read inside the reading (a handler or warning that waits on processes) adds its bodies behind them, and a read cut short by an error or quit leaves the rest to a timer, where GNU leaves the unread bytes in the buffer until the next output; GNU's `jsonrpc--in-process-filter` guard, declared and never bound, is gone. Each message still has a timer of its own, as GNU's, but the messages wait in a queue on the process and each timer hands over the oldest, so two reads' messages are received in order even when their timers share a time, where GNU's bind each timer to its message. A JSON-RPC process must be a pipe or a socket, as a pty's line discipline (echo, CR/LF) breaks the byte protocol and its output is not framed: `jsonrpc-process-connection` refuses a pty before taking it over, with an error naming the fix, and a process it made for the connection goes with its stderr buffer. Eglot binds `tramp-pipe-process` (`lisp/net/tramp.el`, Romacs's) around its server's `make-process`, in place of GNU's `tramp-use-ssh-controlmaster-options` `suppress` (bug#61350), and runs the server's command as given, without GNU's `stty raw` wrapper. For that call only, TRAMP gives a pipe at both ends: before any backend runs, `tramp-file-name-handler` checks the route and makes the local process a pipe whatever `:connection-type` says; only the shapes of the ssh, scp and rsync methods pass (tramp-sh's ssh, the host its last login argument with no `--` before it, direct asynchronous arguments `-t -t`), on one hop, and every other route (sudo, sudoedit, sshfs, androidsu, containers, multi-hop, adb, smb, a user's method of another shape) is refused naming what works. The process is TRAMP's direct asynchronous one with `-T` among ssh's options in place of the method's `-t -t`, sharing TRAMP's own connection as a client (`ControlMaster=no` and its `ControlPath`, as `tramp-use-connection-share` gives it), never as its master, so a login TRAMP's connection authenticated, by password or MFA too, is not asked for again; a user's `suppress` stays theirs. With sharing off or suppressed, or no TRAMP connection open, the server's ssh must log in alone, without a prompt (keys or the user's own ssh multiplexing). Direct asynchronous processes made without `tramp-pipe-process` are GNU's. Checked over `/ssh:localhost:` to a small LSP server on stdio: eglot connects with no new TCP connection to port 22, a request round-trips, a 1 MiB reply arrives whole, framing is native with no tty, shutdown exits 0; an ssh forbidden every way of logging in gets in through TRAMP's connection and dies without it; with 8 MiB of the server's output waiting unread on the shared connection, 60 synchronous TRAMP file operations take half a second (bug#61350's case) and the output then arrives whole.
