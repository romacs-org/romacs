## Observable by a package: GNU Lisp written for the engine

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LISP-JSONRPC-FRAMING | jsonrpc.el's Content-Length framing is the process layer's | `jsonrpc--process-filter` inserts each chunk into the connection's ` *NAME output*` buffer, searches it for a `Content-Length` header (within 100 characters) and parses the body from the buffer once it has come, keeping the expected length in the connection's `-expected-bytes` slot | `jsonrpc-process-connection` sets `(set-process-framing PROC 'content-length)` (`lisp/vm/subprocess.el`), so the process layer splits the frames as it reads (`crates/romacs-vm/src/framing.rs`) and the filter is called once a read with the bodies it completed, each parsed by `jsonrpc--json-read`, now a function of the body string. The ` *NAME output*` buffer is still made and still ends the filter's work when killed, but no output is inserted in it; the `-expected-bytes` slot is gone; a header block with no readable Content-Length (a server's stray output) is skipped, where GNU's search may skip it or wait; one over 8 KiB ends the framing of that connection with a warning. Messages, their order, `:jsonrpc-json`, the timers they are handed to and the re-entrancy guard are GNU's | Per message through `cat`: 68,200 instructions before, 28,100 after (10,000 messages of 185 bytes); one 1 MiB message 8.2 M before, 1.5 M after. One mechanism frames every protocol client | <!-- symbols: jsonrpc--process-filter, jsonrpc--json-read, jsonrpc-process-connection, set-process-framing -->
