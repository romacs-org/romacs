## Observable by a package: GNU Lisp written for the engine

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LISP-JSONRPC-FRAMING | jsonrpc.el's Content-Length framing is the process layer's | `jsonrpc--process-filter` inserts each chunk into the connection's ` *NAME output*` buffer, searches it for a `Content-Length` header (within 100 characters) and parses the body from the buffer once it has come, keeping the expected length in the connection's `-expected-bytes` slot | `jsonrpc-process-connection` sets `(set-process-framing PROC 'content-length)` (`lisp/vm/subprocess.el`), so the process layer splits the frames as it reads (`crates/romacs-vm/src/framing.rs`) and the filter is called once a read with the bodies it completed, each parsed by `jsonrpc--json-read`, now a function of the body string. The ` *NAME output*` buffer is still made and still ends the filter's work when killed, but no output is inserted in it; the `-expected-bytes` slot is gone; a header block with no readable Content-Length (a server's stray output) is skipped, where GNU's search may skip it or wait; one over 8 KiB ends the framing of that connection with a warning. The bodies wait in a queue on the process and are read in order: a read inside the reading (a handler or warning that waits on processes) adds its bodies behind them, and a read cut short by an error or quit leaves the rest to a timer, where GNU leaves the unread bytes in the buffer until the next output; GNU's `jsonrpc--in-process-filter` guard, declared and never bound, is gone. Each message still has a timer of its own, as GNU's, but the messages wait in a queue on the process and each timer hands over the oldest, so two reads' messages are received in order even when their timers share a time, where GNU's bind each timer to its message. A JSON-RPC process must be a pipe or a socket, as a pty's line discipline (echo, CR/LF) breaks the byte protocol and its output is not framed: `jsonrpc-process-connection` refuses a pty with an error naming the fix. Eglot makes its server's process in `jsonrpc-with-remote-pipes`, which lets that one `make-process` see a connection-local `tramp-direct-async-process` t after the user's own profiles (theirs still win), so a remote server over an ssh-based TRAMP method is TRAMP's direct asynchronous process; and `tramp-handle-make-process` leaves ssh's `-t` (a remote pty) out of a direct asynchronous process asked for with `:connection-type 'pipe`, which GNU's TRAMP passes, so the pipe holds at both ends. A method with no direct asynchronous process gets the error. Checked with eglot over `/ssh:localhost:` to a small LSP server: it connects, a request round-trips, the process is framed natively, and it shuts down. Messages and `:jsonrpc-json` are GNU's | Per message through `cat`: 68,200 instructions before, 30,900 after (10,000 messages of 185 bytes); one 1 MiB message 8.2 M before, 1.7 M after, and read 4 KiB at a time 48.8 M before, 8.4 M after.
