## Observable by a package: GNU Lisp written for the engine

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LISP-JSON-READ | json.el's `json-read` reads through the native parser | json.el reads a value a character at a time with `following-char` and `forward-char`, building strings by `concat` of their characters, through `json-read-string`, `json-read-number`, `json-read-keyword`, `json-read-array`, `json-read-object` and `json-add-to-object` | `lisp/romacs/romacs-json.el`, loaded after every load of json.el (`romacs-follow-gnu-file`, `romacs-loadup-bootstrap.el`), defines `json-read` again and takes a new snapshot of json.el's reader functions: when json.el's settings are ones `json-parse-buffer` builds and json.el's reader functions are its own (a multibyte buffer, no element hooks, no reader function redefined, advised or bound by `cl-letf`, the standard `obarray` current, `json-special-chars` as json.el defines it, symbols for `json-null` and `json-false` (a string, vector or list of the caller's is returned itself and never entered, by json.el's reader), `json-array-type` vector or list, `json-object-type` with its default key type or the same one named), the native parser reads the value, its all-ASCII strings are made unibyte as json.el makes them, and after a number or keyword the blanks are skipped and one that runs on is left to json.el. Every other case, and any value the native parser refuses, json.el's own reader reads from the same point, so json.el's errors and leniencies stay. **To a package:** `symbol-file` and `find-function` name romacs-json.el and the docstring is Romacs's wording; a refused value is read twice, once by each reader. Accepted and pinned by `romacs-json-read-keeps-its-recorded-differences`: a value the native parser reads leaves the match data as they were, where json.el's reader leaves those of its last `looking-at`; a document the native parser refuses after an object key leaves that key's symbol interned, where json.el's reader interns a key only once its value is read; nesting deeper than json.el's reader reaches under `max-lisp-eval-depth` (about three frames a level) is read, up to about one frame a level: in batch, at the default 1,600, 1,400 levels against json.el's 600. The native parser itself (`src/prim/leaf/json.rs`) keeps its open containers on a heap stack and refuses past 10,000 levels by a counter (`json-object-too-deep`); the walk that makes ASCII strings unibyte recurses in Lisp, so `max-lisp-eval-depth` bounds the fast path first. Past either bound the native result is dropped and json.el's reader signals as GNU's does (`excessive-lisp-nesting`, its point a few levels from where json.el's reader alone stops, as the frames a level differ): no depth reaches the machine's own stack, and `romacs-json-read-refuses-deep-nesting-as-gnus` reads 100,000 nested arrays and objects. Values, strings' multibyteness, hash tables' order, point afterwards and errors at their points are GNU's: `test/romacs/lisp/romacs-json-tests.el` reads GNU's `json-read` from json.el and compares 1,500 generated documents, a third damaged, under every object, array and key type, with `json-false` and `json-null` symbols or the caller's strings, vectors and lists (returned themselves and left unchanged), a let-bound `obarray` and a changed `json-special-chars`, narrowed or not, in multibyte and unibyte buffers, and checks that a changed reader function and the element hooks are called as json.el calls them; GNU's own json-tests.el passes, `test-json-read` (which binds the reader functions by `cl-letf`) included | A JSON document cost two or three primitive calls a character: 200 objects (23,000 characters) by `json-read-from-string`, 134 M instructions before, 15.6 M after, pinned GNU's `.elc` 30.4 M.
