## Observable by a package: ISCII (IS 13194) coding

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VM2-ISCII-RAW-BYTES | `in-is13194-devanagari` (alias `devanagari`, `lisp/language/indian.el`), `indian-ucs-to-iscii-region`, `indian-iscii-to-ucs-region` and the IS 13194 table (`lisp/language/ind-util.el`) | An `iso-2022` coding system over the `indian-is13194` charset, whose characters lie above #x10FFFF; its conversions map Devanagari to and from those characters through `ucs-devanagari-to-is13194-alist`. GNU's decode is not the inverse of its encode for 12 of the table's 128 characters: the bytes of U+090C (a6 e9), U+093D (ea e9), U+0944 (df e9), U+0950 (a1 e9), U+0960 (aa e9), U+0961 (a7 e9), U+0962 (db e9) and U+0963 (e5 e9) decode as a base and nukta (U+0907 U+093C, U+0964 U+093C, U+0943 U+093C, U+0901 U+093C, U+090B U+093C, U+0908 U+093C, U+093F U+093C, U+094B U+093C), U+0965 (ea ea) as two dandas, U+0951 (f0 b5) and U+0952 (f0 b8) keep a charset character above #x10FFFF, and U+0911 (b2) decodes as U+090D, whose byte is ae. | Buffers hold Unicode and raw bytes, so the table maps each character to its ISCII bytes, as GNU's encoder emits them; its conversions (same names) produce and read those bytes as raw bytes. The coding system keeps GNU's `iso-2022` definition, so its coding category and `coding-system-priority-list` stay GNU's; while reading and writing in it are refused (below), only the conversions use the table, and romacs#336, which lets them run, settles how its decoder hands them the bytes. Encoding is GNU's byte for byte over all 128 characters. Decoding takes the longest table entry at each position and gives those 12 characters back: in ISCII nukta after those bases names the special letter, and the Unicode Standard 15.0 (section 12.1) has modern ISCII-1991 text convert to Unicode and back without loss; it lists no nukta pairs, and the pairs are GNU's own table's, none checked against IS 13194:1991. A consonant with nukta (U+0958..U+095E) decodes as the consonant and U+093C, as GNU's does: its canonical decomposition, which NFC keeps. A character with no ISCII form (encoded as ASCII text such as "[U+0900]") is not decoded back. Reading or writing a file in ISCII is refused until coding systems run conversion hooks (romacs#336), with the machine's error. | **Visible to the user**: ISCII text no longer goes through characters above #x10FFFF, which no buffer here holds; `test/romacs/lisp/language/ind-util-tests.el` holds encoding and decoding to pinned GNU's recording (`test/romacs/lisp/language/ind-util-gnu.eld`) entry by entry. | <!-- symbols: in-is13194-devanagari, indian-ucs-to-iscii-region, indian-iscii-to-ucs-region, indian-regexp-of-hashtbl-keys, indian-is13194-decode-exclusions, in-is13194-post-read-conversion, in-is13194-pre-write-conversion -->
