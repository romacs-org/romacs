## Observable by a package: isolates and frozen values

| # | Scope | GNU | VM2 | Reason / evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VM2-ISOLATE-MEMORY | A job's memory budget (`isolate-job-memory-headroom`) | `memory_full` (alloc.c) signals `memory-signal-data`, "Memory exhausted--use M-x save-some-buffers then exit and restart Emacs", in the code whose allocation failed, catchable by its handlers. | A child forked for one job runs under the budget its zygote was made with, from its fork for its whole life. A collection that finds live growth past it, wherever it ran (an ordinary poll, a cleanup's entry, an explicit `garbage-collect`, a finalizer or `post-gc-hook`, the job's end), ends the child at once (`isolate::job_memory_exit`: native, no Lisp, no unwinding), after one `memory` frame to its spawner; the exit status is fixed (87) but a diagnostic only. The spawner replies for the job with the condition `isolate-memory-exhausted`, its data `(N)`, the child's budget in bytes from its zygote record, as data and not only text: its `error-conditions` are `(isolate-memory-exhausted error)`, so an `error` handler catches it, and its message reads "Memory exhausted: the job grew past its budget, isolate-job-memory-headroom". It is settled once, with the callbacks of any other error reply; a result already accepted stays, and only a job's child gets this reply. Should the frame not fit its socket within a second, the death reads as an ordinary `isolate-died`: honest, less specific. The job's handlers, finalizers, `post-gc-hook` and cleanups never see it. The commands a worker starts (`romacs-process-start`, subprocesses) are reported to its spawner as they start, best effort; at any death of the worker, this one included, the spawner sends SIGHUP to each reported group, as the worker's own exit does. A command it could not report (no room within the bound, the instant between its start and its report) or cannot be reached (no pidfd), or one that ignores SIGHUP, outlives it, as after a crash. A long-lived worker has no budget. | A job is a disposable child: its parent, not its own code, owns the over-budget decision, so no handler, cleanup, finalizer or hook can take or lose it (the OS model of a process over its limit). Shipped isolate users catch no memory error. Tests: `crates/romacs-vm/tests/isolate_zygotes.rs` (the budget test, the stopped zygote, the commands, a result accepted first, an interrupt left unread), `crates/romacs-vm/tests/isolates.rs` (a worker exiting 87 is an ordinary death), `isolate::tests` (a dying peer's last frames). | <!-- symbols: isolate-job-memory-headroom, isolate-memory-exhausted, isolate-spawn, isolate-await, romacs-process-start -->
