## Observable by a package

| # | mechanism | GNU | romacs | why |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| VM2-LAYER-ANIMATION | Layers animate on the host | GNU shows, moves, resizes and deletes child frames at once; a toolkit may animate their windows, Emacs does not | The display animates child frames from the host frame clock, never from Lisp (`romacs-layer-animation`, `rlisp/romacs-layer.el`: t, `reduced` or nil; nil when the file is not loaded): a layer fades in over 140ms (ease-out cubic) with an 8px slide from the edge it is anchored at (its `romacs-layer-edge` parameter), fades out over 90ms, tweens its rect over 80ms when its size changes, its content placed where it settles and clipped to the moving rect, and its selection band (the row its `romacs-layer-selection` parameter names, which `romacs-complete` sets) glides between rows over 60ms; `reduced` keeps the fades without the slide. Lisp sees each layer's final geometry and state at once (`frame-position`, `frame-text-height`, `romacs-layers`); no Lisp runs for an animation frame; a key ends every animation in flight. A display on a software adapter (every headless harness and GUI test) draws none, so screenshots and parity do not depend on frame timing, unless `ROMACS_LAYER_ANIMATION=1` (`harness/layer_anim_live.py`). `tests/child_frames.rs` (romacs-layout), `render_thread/child_frames` tests | **User-observable** only: for at most 140ms a layer's pixels lag where Lisp put it; a screenshot taken in that time shows the animation | Deliberate Romacs default (design note ui-layer §j; Zed gpui's `Animation` and easing are the concept reference, no code copied) | <!-- symbols: romacs-layer-animation romacs-layer-show -->
