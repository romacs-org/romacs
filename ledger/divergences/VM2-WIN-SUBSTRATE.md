| VM2-WIN-SUBSTRATE | One terminal; top-level frames where the host shows them | GNU has traced frame/window/terminal objects (`src/frame.h:145`, `src/window.h:100`, `src/termhooks.h:475`) and supports multiple devices and frame lifecycles | VM2 allocates traced Frame/Terminal identities; a Frame owns its root, selected and minibuffer windows and a top-level one its native geometry, and every Window points back to its Frame. One terminal, not a TTY, holds every frame, as one pgtk display does. Where the host shows more than one top-level frame (`HostInput::top_level_frames`), `make-frame` without a `parent-frame` makes one, over a mini-window of its own or one it borrows (`(minibuffer . nil)` or a window), its faces copies of the selected frame's; `delete-frame` keeps frame.c's checks and its `mru` choice of the next selected frame; `next-frame` and `previous-frame` follow `candidate_frame`; a key or a focus change from another frame is read after its `switch-frame`, a resize sizes and a closed window deletes the frame it names (`tests/top_frames.el` matches pinned GNU X11). The GUI's host shows one top-level frame until its display paints more than one window (romacs#159), and there a `parent-frame` is still required. Not implemented: minibuffer-only frames (refused), `minibuffer-follows-selected-frame` (an active minibuffer stays in its frame, as with the option nil), faces set on one top-level frame alone (it paints with the selected frame's), device switching and saved recursive-minibuffer selection. `window-list`, `get-buffer-window`, `window-combination-limit`, `terminal-list`, `tty-top-frame` and `last-nonminibuffer-frame` are callable over this actual graph | One display; the GUI's second window waits on its display; no claim of multi-device parity.
