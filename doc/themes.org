#+title: Making a theme

A Romacs theme is an ordinary Emacs theme.  You write a short named
palette; Romacs derives the rest, every face included.  =load-theme=,
=enable-theme= and =disable-theme= treat the result as they treat any
other theme.

* A theme in ten colours

=bg=, =fg= and the six hues are all a theme needs.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'romacs-theme)

(def-romacs-theme harbour "Slate water and buoy paint."
  :mode dark
  ((bg "#14191f") (fg "#d5dde6")
   (red "#e0727a") (green "#8fc08a") (yellow "#dcbc7a")
   (blue "#6fa8dc") (magenta "#b892d8") (cyan "#68b8c0")
   (accent yellow)
   (selection (romacs-theme-blend bg blue 0.3))))
#+end_src

Save it as =harbour-theme.el= in =custom-theme-directory=, and it shows up
in =load-theme= like any other theme.

* The palette

Each row is =(SLOT VALUE)=.  VALUE is a colour, the name of another slot,
or a form over the rows before it.  Name any slot you care about and leave
the others:

- surfaces: =bg= =bg-dark= =bg-darker= =bg-alt=
- text: =fg= =fg-dim= =fg-light= =fg-bright= =muted=
- hues: =red= =green= =yellow= =blue= =magenta= =cyan= =orange= =brown= and
  their =bright-= forms
- interface: =accent= =selection= =cursor= =border= =bg-mode-line= =bg-region=
- code: =keyword= =function= =variable= =type= =constant= =string= =comment=
  =builtin= =doc= =preprocessor=
- the terminal's sixteen: =ansi-0= to =ansi-15=

=romacs-theme-slots= lists them all.  Any name of the Modus face table, such
as =bg-hl-line= or =fg-heading-1=, works too.

* Colour helpers

These work in Oklab, so a blend keeps its perceived lightness.

- =(romacs-theme-lighten COLOR AMOUNT)= and =(romacs-theme-darken COLOR AMOUNT)=
  move lightness by AMOUNT, 0 to 1.
- =(romacs-theme-blend A B AMOUNT)= is the colour AMOUNT of the way from A to B.
- =(romacs-theme-color SLOT)= reads a slot.  While a theme is being defined it
  reads that theme; otherwise it reads the enabled theme.

* Changing single faces

The optional list after the palette overrides faces.  Attributes merge over
what the palette gives the face, and a colour attribute may name a slot.

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(def-romacs-theme harbour-keel "A warmer keyword, a boxed string."
  ((bg "#14191f") (fg "#d5dde6")
   (red "#e0727a") (green "#8fc08a") (yellow "#dcbc7a")
   (blue "#6fa8dc") (magenta "#b892d8") (cyan "#68b8c0"))
  ((font-lock-keyword-face :foreground yellow :weight bold)
   (font-lock-string-face :box (:line-width 1 :color green))
   (hl-line :background (romacs-theme-blend bg cyan 0.12))))
#+end_src

Variants that share one list can branch on the theme's mode:
=(show-paren-match &dark (:underline t) &light (:overline t))=.

The list after that sets variables, as =(VARIABLE FORM)= rows with the slots
bound.

* Style and mode

=:mode= is =dark= or =light=, read from =bg= when left out.  =:family= groups a
theme's variants.  =:style= names its material style, =flat= or =modern=, or is a
property list of style tokens.

* Readability

Romacs keeps text readable: each face's foreground is checked against the
background it shows on, at a contrast of 4.5:1, or 3:1 for de-emphasised text
such as comments.  A foreground that falls short moves just far enough.  An
override in the faces list is your own choice and stays as you wrote it.

See what a theme resolved to and what the floors moved:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(require 'romacs-theme-roles)

(let ((rows (romacs-theme-rows 'harbour)))
  (list (cdr (assq 'keyword (plist-get rows :palette)))
        (length (plist-get rows :faces))
        (plist-get rows :floors)))
#+end_src

=romacs-theme-palette= returns the resolved palette of an enabled theme, and
=romacs-theme-uncovered= lists the faces a theme does not reach.
